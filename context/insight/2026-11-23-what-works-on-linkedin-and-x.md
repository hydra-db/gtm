## Claim

HydraDB's biggest social wins came from loud, contrarian launch moments (the "kill vector databases"
launch), amplified by a high-status repost and launch amplification. Day-to-day LinkedIn posts sit around
2,500 impressions and only lift with sharper hooks, video, weekday PT-morning timing, real first-hour
comments, and personal (not corporate) stories. Overall hypothesis; individual lines say when something
was seen by more than one person.

## Evidence

**First launch, 2026-03-12 (public funding and launch announcement)**

- Internal: team discussion, 2026-03-12. The LinkedIn post titled "We've raised $6.5M to kill vector
  databases" neared 1,400 likes and 80k impressions. Launch message from Nishkarsh: "huge day - first time
  announcing HydraDB to the world. I've been waiting to build something in search for my entire life and
  im finally able to do it."
- Internal: sales notes, 2026-05-08. Same launch on X: "went viral with 3.7M views on our first launch
  itself" (used in an outreach template). Team-reported, no screenshot: hypothesis. Do not add the X and
  LinkedIn numbers together.
- Internal: team discussion, 2026-03-12. The provocative copy "definitely seems to be working from a
  virality approach".
- Internal: founder note, 2026-03-09. Launch brief decisions: the CTA "has to be use us or something", and
  the launch would not mention the conference at all.
- Internal: team discussion, March to August 2026. Others noticed: another founder asked how HydraDB got
  its impressions on X; a peer said Nishkarsh has "done amazing social viral launches". Team takeaway:
  "Honestly I didn't realize how big of a deal X virality is earlier for anything tech/AI".

**The founder's launch playbook (Internal: founder note, 2026-04-28)**

- Jeff Dean publicly reposted the launch video, and many others followed; "that is what made it go
  viral". Keep it factual: he reposted; claim nothing beyond that.
- "a breakdown of all components would be: 1. a hook in the video 2. enticing title 3. and making sure to
  launch 7am"
- Reach worked best as a mix of amplification and the team's own networks, with own networks the larger
  share.
- "Post the 1M impressions mark is when you start seeing actual traction and usage for your product."
- Internal: founder note, 2026-11-06: "we need initial traction to go super duper viral".

**Amplification on X, March to June 2026: views yes, conversations no**

- Internal: team discussion, 2026-05-08. The first 2 launch videos crossed 1.9M views.
- Internal: team discussion, 2026-04-13. Cadence: "one big launch moment per month instead of the mini
  series ... target 1.5M impressions".
- Internal: founder note, 2026-03-04: "Over time I want us to be relying more on organic channels".
- Internal: team discussion, 2026-06-05. Articles posted by third-party accounts got weak comments, because
  they reached mostly those accounts' own follower clusters unless they went viral. Seen by two people.

**Other wins with numbers**

- Internal: founder note, 2026-07-11. One founder LinkedIn post: two people from his network asked for
  HydraDB trials.
- Internal: team discussion, 2026-11-04. A team member's post: 1,000+ impressions and about 30 reactions
  in 7 hours on about 1,500 followers; see [[insight/2026-11-04-founder-and-team-posts-beat-blogs]].
- Internal: team discussion, 2026-11-14. Hackathon announcement post: "5K impressions and counting."
- Internal: team discussion, 2026-04-16. A teammate went viral more than once with posts riffing on
  research news (Google TurboQuant, the ReasoningBank paper).

**Baseline and what fell flat**

- Internal: team discussion, 2026-09-06. Average LinkedIn impressions about 2,500 per post in early
  September. Diagnosis: "1. Hooks, more crispy and psychologically driven 2. More internal content like
  what's happening inside the company and day to day workflow ... 3. More general AI content ...
  Nishkarsh's identity should not only be driven by HydraDB's innovation, progress and methodologies. It
  should be his personal brand".
- Internal: team discussion, 2026-07-22. A longer definitional post ("Your business does not have one
  agreed definition...") got slightly less engagement; response was to cut length and test timing.
- Internal: team discussion, 2026-11-12. An open-source launch got no social post: "we did selfgraph OSS,
  but posted nothing about it".
- Internal: founder note, 2026-08-25. Engagement quality: Nishkarsh flagged "1.5k impressions on 300 likes"
  as a weird ratio. Some early engagement came from the team and its networks boosting posts. Do not
  present engagement counts as purely organic.

**Format and timing**

- Internal: team discussion, 2026-08-23. Video: a claim that impressions had compounded on podcast and
  video posts. Disputed the same day: "I don't think that's possible, can you share the data".
- Internal: founder note, 2026-07-14 and 2026-07-28. Founder interview series: weekly videos with other
  founders from Nishkarsh's account, as a "smaller video on my linkedin that can be linked back to the main
  youtube video via comments".
- Timing, seen by two people: weekend posts "get overridden by newer posts posted earlier on Monday"
  (Internal: team discussion, 2026-07-17); "only post PST AM" (Internal: founder note, 2026-07-27);
  "better engagement on weekdays around 10-10:30am PT rather than earlier than that" (Internal: team
  discussion, 2026-08-01).
- Tagging: tagging companies raises reach (Internal: team discussion, 2026-07-10), but "Company tagging is
  okay but no personal name tagging pls. At all." (Internal: founder note, 2026-08-12, supersedes the July
  plan to tag founders).
- Internal: founder note, 2026-08-01. X leads LinkedIn: "LinkedIn audience is a lagging indicator of
  what's happening on Twitter".
- Internal: team discussion, 2026-11-21. First hour on X: "a real comment beats an emoji ... in the first
  hour; that's when X decides how far it travels".
- Internal: founder note, 2026-11-20. Video style: "i think we're doing too much in every video. sometimes
  doing less is actually the best way to communicate clarity" and "less is more" (2026-11-22); see
  [[insight/2026-11-20-automated-video-engine-on-x]].

**Content mix and cadence**

- Internal: team discussion, 2026-08-24. Agreed mix: "1. AI news/ Graph related stuff 2. Internal insights
  of the company like sharing what we have achieved, failed, and learnings ... 3. General Nishkarsh's
  personal experience building AI startup (once in a month or something because it can go viral)". Later
  (Internal: founder note, 2026-11-02): launches are "the 20% and the rest 80% would be learnings, POVs,
  contrarian takes, etc."
- Internal: team discussion, 2026-08-05. Content session: personal accounts "stay broad/open ('graph
  engineering') for ~2 months to build authority as 'the graph/ontology people'"; "for an infra company
  that means showing engineering experiments/learnings" rather than visionary thought leadership; business
  buyers need case studies, benchmark reports and one-pagers.
- Internal: founder note, 2026-05-27. Earlier ask: news explainers and regular content and blogs, the way
  memory-category players post on X.
- Internal: founder note, 2026-10-15. Paper and event captions: "please don't write generic stuff. break
  down the paper, discussions, and 3 main learnings from that discussions + any FAQs answered".
- Internal: founder note, 2026-11-19. Planned series: "im going to post about real world events and help
  people predict outcomes using 'conspiracy boards' modeling all world events i find interesting and
  talking about the related entities in that".
- Internal: team discussion, 2026-10-01 and 2026-11-19. Cadence: personal account 3 posts a week, company
  2 to 3, cross-posted to X; scheduled Monday, Wednesday, Friday.

## Implication

Default weekly posts will not go viral on their own. A post earns reach when it picks a fight (vector
databases, similarity is not relevance), rides a news moment already trending on X, includes a short video
or clip, ships on a weekday PT morning, gets real comments in the first hour, and is specific and personal
(a number from inside the company, a mistake, a contrarian take on AI news). Generic, AI-sounding product
copy is both low reach and a brand risk. Big numbers come from concentrated launch moments, about one per
month, not a drip of mini launches. For LinkedIn, organic founder voice is the channel.

## Use in messaging

- Contrarian framing works for this audience: the "kill vector databases" launch is the proof. Reuse the
  shape, not the exact line, and never name a competitor.
- News-jacking (a lab's memory release, a framework's download numbers, a research paper) is the repeatable
  format.
- Founder-lesson posts: "Our first launch hit 3.7M views on X. Luck had little to do with it." (say "the
  team counted", the figure is unverified), and "Amplification buys views, not conversations." Keep any
  vendor names and amounts out.
- Do not describe launch reach or engagement as purely organic.
- Scheduling notes (weekday 10 to 10:30am PT, Monday/Wednesday/Friday) are for the drafter, not post content.
- Never tag individuals; tagging companies is fine.
