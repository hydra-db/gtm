## Claim

A personal LinkedIn post gets in front of the right people in hours; a blog takes a month. One founder
post produced two trial requests from his network.

## Evidence

- Internal: founder note, 2026-07-11. Nishkarsh: two people from his network reached out asking for
  HydraDB trials after one LinkedIn post.
- Internal: team discussion, 2026-11-04. A team member with about 1,500 followers: a post got 1,000+
  impressions and about 30 reactions in 7 hours; "a blog would take a month to get here organically. ...
  For most of you the audience is already relevant. So when you publish, the impact shows up much faster."
- Internal: team discussion, 2026-11-03. Build-in-public program: team members post their own work on X
  and LinkedIn for 3 weeks, "Not HydraDB marketing. Your own work: what you built, what broke, what you
  found interesting in agents, retrieval, graphs, databases or infra. About 1 in 5 posts can be a HydraDB
  launch." Goal: 2 posts per person per week.
- Internal: team discussion, 2026-09-15. Other startups ask HydraDB to engage with their launches with "a
  meaningful comment longer than 10 words".

## Implication

For a technical audience, the founder's and engineers' own accounts are the distribution. Company-page and
blog content is slower and lower intent.

## Use in messaging

Post: "We asked every engineer to post about what broke, not about HydraDB. One post did in 7 hours what a
blog does in a month."
